DO BUSINESS CARDS MATTER IN 2026?

Yes, and for a reason that has nothing to do with nostalgia. Almost
everything else in your marketing is something people look at. A card is
something they pick up, turn over, and put in a pocket. For most
prospects it is the only piece of your work they will ever hold in their
hands. So the question was never whether cards still matter. It is
whether yours is worth keeping.

THE SAME HANDSHAKE, TWO WAYS
Version one is a 14pt card, one side printed and the other blank, with
every way to reach you on the front. Version two is the same
information at 16pt, soft-touch, with one line on the back saying what
you actually do. Same handshake, same thirty-second conversation. The
difference is what is still in their pocket that evening. A card does
not win the work. It sets the expectation the work then confirms.

WHAT THE RESEARCH ACTUALLY SHOWS
Sappi North America worked with neuroscientist Dr. David Eagleman on a
reading experiment. Participants read the same brochure three ways: on
high-quality coated paper, on low-grade uncoated paper, and on a screen.
The premium-paper group had the best comprehension and recall, the most
favorable impression of the company, and the highest likelihood to
recommend it. A week later, brand-name recall in that group ran about
3:1 over the others. Two limits worth being straight about: Sappi
manufactures paper, so the finding is not disinterested, and the study
measured brochures, not cards. It establishes the mechanism, not a
number you can apply to a card order.
  Source: A Communicator's Guide to the Neuroscience of Touch, Sappi
  North America with Dr. David Eagleman, 2015.

THE FOUR DECISIONS YOU ARE ALREADY MAKING
  Weight - 14pt is standard. At 16pt a card stops feeling like paper and
  starts feeling like an object. It is the largest perceptual jump for
  the smallest money.
  The edge - nobody thinks about it and everybody feels it. A clean cut,
  a painted edge, or a rounded corner is what a thumb finds while you
  are still talking.
  The finish - soft-touch changes a card more than any color decision
  will. Gloss photographs well and is the most common finish.
  Matte reads quieter and takes a pen. Pick one embellishment, not
  three.
  The back - usually blank, already paid for. One line of what you do, a
  QR to the work, or a short list of services beats white.
  For the record: 250 double-sided cards start around $60, and finishes
  and heavier stocks move up from there.

ONE HONEST CATCH
A card only works when it feels intentional. We would love to print you
a good one, and we will be straight with you about what it will and will
not do. A card cannot make the case for you, and it is not always the
right tool. If your work is visual and the conversation happens at a
table, a card is too small; that is what a brochure is for. A card with room
to breathe reads as considered. Deciding what to leave off is what makes
everything you kept land harder.
