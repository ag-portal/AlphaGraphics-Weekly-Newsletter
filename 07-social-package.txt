DAY 3 - WEDNESDAY  ·  THE RESEARCH
Frame: 03  |  Photo: none (numbers + source line on the image)
ALT: Graphic reading "3 to 1 brand-name recall a week later, premium
paper over screen," with the Sappi and Eagleman source line beneath.

FACEBOOK
There is one study worth citing here. Sappi North America worked with
neuroscientist Dr. David Eagleman on a reading experiment: the same
brochure on high-quality coated paper, on low-grade uncoated paper, and
on a screen. The premium-paper group had the best comprehension and
recall and the most favorable impression of the company, and a week
later brand-name recall in that group ran about 3:1 over the others. Two
limits worth being straight about - Sappi manufactures paper, so the
finding is not disinterested, and the study measured brochures, not
business cards. It shows the mechanism, not a number to apply to a card
order.
Source: A Communicator's Guide to the Neuroscience of Touch, Sappi North
America with Dr. David Eagleman, 2015.

DAY 4 - THURSDAY  ·  THE FOUR DECISIONS
Frame: 04  |  Photo: w10-gold-foil.jpg (design concept)
ALT: Design concept - gold foil logo stamped on a soft-touch white
business card.

FACEBOOK
Four decisions do most of the work on a business card, and none of them
is design. Weight: 14pt is standard, and at 16pt a card stops feeling
like paper and starts feeling like an object. The edge: nobody thinks
about it and everybody feels it. The finish: soft-touch changes a card
more than any color decision will, and one embellishment beats three.
The back: usually blank, already paid for, and one line of what you do
beats white.

INSTAGRAM
Four decisions, none of them design. Weight - 16pt is where a card stops
feeling like paper. The edge - nobody thinks about it, everybody feels
it. The finish - pick one, not three. The back - it is already paid for
and it is usually blank.

DAY 5 - FRIDAY  ·  THE HONEST CATCH
Frame: 05  |  Photo: w10-embossed.jpg (design concept)
ALT: Design concept - blind embossed folded business card on olive green
uncoated stock.

FACEBOOK
One honest catch: a card only works when it feels intentional. We would
love to print you a good one, and we will be straight with you about
what it will and will not do. A card cannot make the case for you, and
it is not always the right tool - if your work is visual and the
conversation happens at a table, a card is too small. What a considered
card does is make one argument well: somebody here decided.
