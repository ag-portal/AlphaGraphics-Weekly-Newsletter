WEEK 10 - SOURCES

USED IN THE COPY

1. A Communicator's Guide to the Neuroscience of Touch
   Sappi North America with Dr. David Eagleman, 2015.
   Written with Lana Rigsby of Rigsby Hull.
   Finding used: participants read the same brochure for a fictitious
   company on high-quality coated paper, low-grade uncoated paper, and
   on screen. Premium-paper readers had the best comprehension and
   recall, the most favorable impression of the company, and the highest
   likelihood to recommend it. Brand-name recall a week later ran about
   3:1 over the other groups.
   Caveat DISCLOSED IN COPY: Sappi manufactures paper.

2. Weight-to-importance effect (heavy clipboard studies)
   Standard psychophysics / embodied-cognition finding, replicated.
   Used qualitatively only. No percentage attached - deliberately.

3. Hurley, R.A., Holmes, J.L., Tonkin, C.E., Cooksey, K., Rice, J.C.
   "The Effect of Decorative Foil Stamping on Consumer Attention."
   Journal of Applied Packaging Research, Vol. 8, Iss. 2.
   Clemson University; 172 participants; mobile eye tracking in the
   CUshop immersive retail lab.
   Used only to establish that peer-reviewed work on foil and attention
   exists. Packaging, not business cards - stated as such in the copy.
   No figure quoted.
